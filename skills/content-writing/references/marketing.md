# Marketing Content Guidance

Explain why to choose the product, what the reader receives, and what action to take. Value comes from relevance and credibility.

For a long article or unclear direction, first offer a one-sentence thesis, outline, and 2–3 title options for confirmation; then draft.

## Find a Distinctive Angle

Find a non-obvious entry point rather than repeating a surface conclusion the reader has seen many times:

- **Surface:** obvious truths such as “automation matters.” Discard these as the main angle.
- **Tactical:** actionable but generic advice such as “use fewer dashboards.” It may support the article but should not lead it.
- **Strategic / insight:** an unspoken tension, cost, or counterintuitive conclusion the reader senses but has not seen clearly explained. Use it as the through-line.

State in one sentence why now and why this product/company, and make the reader feel recognized.

## Choose a Structure

Choose PAS or AIDA; do not combine them:

- **PAS:** Problem → Agitate → Solution.
- **AIDA:** Attention → Interest → Desire → Action.

## Guidance for Each Stage

1. **Hook / Attention:** Open with a concrete scenario, number, contrast, or question; avoid “In today's…”
2. **Pain / Interest:** Address the real pain of the audience you serve, in their language. Do not generalize to “everyone.”
3. **Solution / Desire:** Explain how it works and why to choose this option, using verifiable evidence.
4. **Credibility:** Use real cases, customer names, data, screenshots, charts, or third-party reviews. Gather them if missing; never invent them.
5. **Reduce risk:** State who the offer is not for and what trial/refund/inquiry requirements apply to reduce friction.
6. **CTA:** Promote one primary action per article. Keep the call to action to one sentence and one direction.

## No Empty Claims

- Without specific data, a case, or testimony, do not claim “increased efficiency,” “industry-leading,” “empowerment,” or similar.
- Avoid paragraphs made only of adjectives. Pair each selling point with what the reader gets or evidence.
- Give a statistic's definition and source; state the base for ratios and percentages.
- React to results instead of merely listing them. Honest costs and inapplicable cases can make a claim more credible than “perfect” promises.

## Voice and Length

- Be persuasive without lecturing. Use short sentences and paragraphs for phone scanning.
- Length does not determine quality; if three sentences are enough, do not use three paragraphs.
- If the CTA appears early, still give sufficient reason in the body for why now and why it is worthwhile.
